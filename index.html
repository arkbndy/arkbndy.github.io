Arka Bandyopadhyay

Computational Quantum Materials · Institute for Theoretical Physics and Astrophysics · Julius-Maximilians-Universität Würzburg · Cluster of Excellence ct.qmat

What fascinates me is the quantum geometry of electronic wavefunctions — the way Berry curvature and the quantum metric become quantities we can measure in the laboratory: non-linear Hall signals, flat bands, transport across oxide interfaces. What I enjoy most is watching an abstract geometric quantity turn into a number a colleague can put on a plot — and that work is always better done alongside experiment. I would be delighted to talk about new collaborations — with theorists and experimentalists alike — anywhere in condensed matter physics and materials theory.

Research programme

Five threads, one question

How do structure, symmetry, quantum geometry, magnetism and electronic correlations come together to create the remarkable properties of quantum materials — and how can we learn to control them? Each thread below explores a different part of this question, linking fundamental physics to measurable phenomena and, ultimately, to new materials and functionalities.

Quantum geometry & unconventional transport

Berry curvature dipoles and the quantum metric, and the beautifully counter-intuitive transverse signals they generate in quantum materials — non-linear Hall, anomalous Nernst, and a refraction effect nobody expected.

8 papers · 3 under review Read more →

Flat bands, non-Hermitian topology & exact lattice methods

Where flat bands come from in kagome and line-graph lattices, and how real-space decimation turns stubborn non-Hermitian numerics into clean analytics.

5 papers · 1 under review Read more →

Dirac materials beyond graphene

Predicting new carbon, silicene and nitride sheets from first principles. As much structural chemistry as band theory: change the bonding — acetylenic links, tetragonal and octagonal rings — and the Dirac physics follows.

19 papers Read more →

Magnetism, oxide interfaces & spintronics

How a small structural tilt at an interface reshapes spin–orbit coupling, what that gains us in tunnelling magnetoresistance, and altermagnetism rewritten in the language of chemistry — with molecular magnets alongside the oxides.

3 papers · 2 under review Read more →

Materials for energy & devices

What the predicted materials are actually good for: lithium storage, supercapacitors, thermoelectrics, photocatalysis, and — with synthetic chemists — redox polymers whose energy density rises with the acceptor conjugation.
